## 2. What matters for the thesis (beyond the article)

The valuation debate is **NOISE**: a low multiple is a market price, not evidence. Kaplan's "the E has to be right" point is the useful one, and Micron's own numbers show what the E depends on:

1. **The E is mostly price.** FQ4-26 DRAM: bits up mid-single digits, **price up high teens**. NAND: bits ~+10%, **price ~+30%**. FQ1-27 guide: single-digit bit growth, gross margin ~86.25% (lowered by ~$1B of incentive-comp and start-up costs), and "a more moderate rate of price increases" for the rest of FY27. This is the same pattern as Sandisk (LE-2026-11-01-SNDK §2.1): pricing still rising, but more slowly. It supports SEM-01 9.0 as held. It does not add to it.
2. **Supply response is accelerating.** FY26 capex was $27.4B. H1-FY27 capex is guided at ~$25B, with H2 higher (analysts on the call: >$50B for FY27). Construction capex is growing faster than equipment capex. ID1 (Idaho) output mid-2027, Singapore HBM packaging early 2027, Tongluo (Taiwan) mid-2027; ID2, Japan and Singapore NAND in late 2028. This is the cyclical risk Kaplan describes, and it runs on Micron's own schedule. **Counterpoint (T2, Bloomberg 11-05, not confirmed by the company):** Micron paused construction of a planned ~$4.5B HBM/DRAM plant in Taoyuan, Taiwan, over a labor dispute. If true, that slows supply growth. Treat it as a single-source report.
3. **Device crowding-out: the best evidence so far for MEMORY-SCARCITY-01.** Micron's own call: PC and mobile industry revenue grows this year "despite potential **double-digit overall unit declines** in both markets." MCBU (mobile and client) revenue rose 14% q/q "driven by higher pricing, **partially offset by lower bit shipments**." This is a DRAM maker saying PC and phone units are falling while it raises prices. It is closer to the MEMORY-SCARCITY-01 escalation condition than the Sandisk consumer-NAND print. **It still does not meet the condition:** the unit declines are a company forecast ("potential"), not verified PC unit data, and there is no OEM margin compression evidence yet. **Weight stays zero.** Next check: IDC/Gartner CQ3 PC units and Apple's memory-cost commentary.
4. **Contract coverage is real but partial.** $150B of RPO at minimum prices, and management says margins at floor prices would still be "meaningfully above any prior cycle peak." Most revenue is still market-priced. The SCAs limit the downside; they do not remove the cycle.
5. **No financing stress at the supplier.** Net cash ~$55.6B excluding customer deposits, $5.2B of debt, BBB+. As with Sandisk, Micron is cash-rich and not part of the circular-financing chain. The deposits ($12.7B) are customer prepayments for supply. They are worth tracking as a measure of how much buyers will pay to secure memory.

**Tape (positioning, not evidence):** MU −7% on Nov 4 (post-midterm AI rotation, CNBC), **−17.46% on Nov 5** (largest one-day drop in a year; SOX −10.5% intraday, S&P 500 −4.4% per PubT), +5.12% on Nov 6. The article's "anti-data-center sentiment after the midterms" is T3 commentary from a portfolio manager. It is not a policy enactment.
