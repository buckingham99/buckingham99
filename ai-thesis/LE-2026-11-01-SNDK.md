## 2. What matters for the thesis (beyond the article)

The split is **NOISE**. The primary documents carry four things the article does not stress:

1. **Price contribution is fading.** FQ1 revenue growth was about 60% volume and 40% price. The FQ2 guide includes only "modest like-for-like price increases." Sequential growth has gone from ~97% to ~51% to 23% to a guided ~13%. That is the NAND analogue of the TrendForce 10-30 spot-retreat counter-signal already logged against SEM-01: scarcity pricing is still rising, but more slowly.
2. **Consumer demand destruction (NAND).** Consumer revenue was $397M, **−29% q/q, "as the market adjusts to higher pricing"**; the low end of Edge contracted. This is direct evidence of consumer crowding-out by memory prices. For MEMORY-SCARCITY-01 it is **adjacent evidence only (zero weight)**: that record escalates only on sustained PC **DRAM** increases plus verified PC unit and margin compression, and Sandisk consumer is NAND retail, not PC units. Next check: Apple FQ4 (Nov 2) memory-cost guide.
3. **The AI pull is real and shifting supply.** Data center is ~half of industry NAND bits (company estimate). Sandisk "allocated more supply in FQ1" to data center, has three U.S. hyperscaler customers, and says its long-term-agreement pipeline "exceeds our supply throughout the end of the decade". Management's **CY2027 view: data center >55% of the NAND market, bit demand ~+40% YoY after more than doubling in 2026**, "enabled by higher AI driven capital spending." The 2027 figure is a forecast, not an enactment. It supports Physical_AI_Demand 9 (held) without adding to it.
4. **No financing stress here.** Operating cash flow $6.4B, capex $378M (3.4% of revenue), adjusted FCF $5.7B (~52% margin), $5.1B cash, no debt, $5B of buybacks in the quarter. This is a cash-rich supplier, not a link in the circular-financing chain.

**Tape (positioning, not evidence):** SNDK −13.47% on Oct 30, the same session as the broad AI rout already recorded (SMH −7.0%, MU −5.3%).
