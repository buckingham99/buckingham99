## 2. What matters for the thesis (beyond the article)

1. **The IG move is in CDS, not (yet) in cash bonds.** Barclays (10-29, via Wallstreetcn / Sina 10-31): the 10 core AI names are 8% of CDX IG constituents but **16% of the index's implied credit risk**, all near the top of their six-month ranges, and the **CDS–bond basis has turned positive** (protection costs more than the bonds' spread). That is hedging demand pushing CDS ahead of cash. The cash IG index was **0.89% OAS on 10-29** (FRED weekly refresh, 10-31). Read: the Financing 8→7 "IG spread gap" trigger is **NOT MET** on the cash index. A single-name widening, however broad, is not an index gap.
2. **Correlation is the real change.** S&P Global (10-13, via Benzinga): AI-exposed tech credit is "starting to trade as a single theme." Grinberg's "no exceptions" says the same. This supports the existing BORDERLINE reason (financing conditions tightening across the stack) rather than adding a new one.
3. **A market for GPU rental-price risk is forming.** Bilateral contracts now (Jump, DRW, SIG), spot indices (Silicon Data H100/B200), exchange futures pending (CME, CFTC). This is the first instrument that would give a **traded price for compute**, the variable that sits under every neocloud contract and under GPU residual values (TW-VF-GUARANTEE-01 context). No observable exists for it yet.
4. **Puts and CDS are positioning, not enactment.** Record CRWV put volume and CDS hedging say what investors fear, not what has happened. Per the standing rule (score enactment, not announcement), they carry **zero weight** on their own.
